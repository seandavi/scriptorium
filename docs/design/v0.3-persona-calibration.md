# v0.3 design memo: `persona-calibration`

*Status: design per issue [#44](https://github.com/seandavi/scriptorium/issues/44). Depends on [#42](https://github.com/seandavi/scriptorium/issues/42) (author-persona docs) and pairs with [#43](https://github.com/seandavi/scriptorium/issues/43) (`voice-profile`). This memo sharpens the design questions; it does not commit to a build.*

## What's ready

- **The convergence-risk frame is established in #42.** Author personas are character-bearing surrogates that close the iteration loop when the real author isn't in the room. Any synthetic-feedback loop converges to whatever the persona rewards — without recalibration, a persona-driven loop optimizes for the persona, not the author. The persona/lens distinction (#42) already keeps `reviewer-simulation` on the lens side; calibration applies on the persona side only.
- **Inspectable-transformation precedent.** Scriptorium's structured-output discipline (`citation-audit`'s per-claim table; `argumentative-flow`'s preservation report) is the shape calibration output should follow: a named structured artifact the author can read and act on, with explicit divergence metrics.
- **Author-side / scarce-time discipline is project-native.** Every conversation-bearing skill is already calibrated to author attention budget (see `meta.guidance_level` and the [`knowledge/conventions/guidance-level.md`](../../knowledge/conventions/guidance-level.md) check-in protocol). Persona-calibration extends the same discipline to the feedback-loop seam.
- **Convergence-failure framing is partly in [`knowledge/peer-review/ai-peer-review-research.md`](../../knowledge/peer-review/ai-peer-review-research.md)** via the Liang 2024 30.85% overlap finding. The same "synthetic feedback ≠ human feedback" gap that motivates reviewer-simulation's *lenses-not-personas* posture applies here in reverse: persona-driven feedback is *more* useful than independent simulation, but only if checkpointed against ground truth.

## What's missing

- **No grounding note on the synthetic-feedback drift / Goodhart literature.** The technical framing scriptorium would want here lives in the broader human-feedback-ML literature: Goodhart's law as applied to RLHF reward models (Gao, Schulman & Hilton 2023 "Scaling laws for reward model overoptimization"; OpenAI's earlier work on reward hacking); the Skalse et al. 2022 NeurIPS reward-hacking taxonomy; the Casper et al. 2023 "Open problems and fundamental limitations of RLHF" survey. Whether to add a dedicated knowledge note depends on how heavily the skill grounds in it (see decision point 1). [TODO verify exact citations before any are reproduced in scriptorium's voice.]
- **No empirical anchor for "5–15 minute author checkpoint is sufficient."** This is a *claim about author cognition* (how much real-author signal does it take to detect persona drift?) that the issue makes without grounding. The cognitive-load / sampling literature might support it; might not.
- **No artifact for persona spec format.** The skill needs a defined `persona-spec` format to read, diff, and write. #42 implementation issue is still TBD.
- **No specified divergence metrics.** Critique-set overlap, rank correlation on critique priority, and agreement on suggested revisions are listed in #44, but the *operationalization* (e.g. Jaccard on flagged sections; Spearman on priority rank) needs to be picked.

## Architectural decision points

### 1. How heavily to ground in the RLHF / reward-hacking literature

Two extremes:

- **Lightly:** name the convergence risk in the SKILL.md, refer to #42 framing, do not introduce ML-side terminology. The user is an author, not an alignment researcher. Costs: looks underjustified to readers who know the literature exists.
- **Heavily:** ground in a dedicated `knowledge/critique-techniques/synthetic-feedback-drift.md` note (or similar) covering Goodhart, reward overoptimization, evaluator-agreement, and the Skalse taxonomy. Costs: scope creep into ML alignment; risk of fabricated or wrong citations if the research is shallow.

**Recommended:** intermediate. Ground in `ai-peer-review-research` (already present; covers evaluator-agreement κ in peer review at length) and add a short subsection or paragraph to `author-role-evidence` covering convergence risk in author-side feedback. **Defer** a dedicated synthetic-feedback-drift knowledge note unless and until building the skill surfaces a real grounding gap. The author-side and reviewer-side κ literature is what scriptorium reaches for first.

### 2. What "5–15 minute author checkpoint" looks like in practice

The scarcity constraint is real and load-bearing. Concrete shapes:

- **Single artifact, focused questions, no re-review from scratch.** Show the author one passage at a time (the most divergent first), the persona's verdict, ask one yes/no/it's-complicated question per passage. 6–10 passages is the practical ceiling for 5–15 minutes.
- **Pre-ranked divergence.** The skill picks the passages with highest persona-vs-author predicted disagreement (using #43's voice-profile as a cross-check, or rank-disagreement across multiple persona instances). Author time is spent where the signal is.
- **Author-as-tiebreaker mode.** Persona and author both flag the same passages; author decides who was right. Cheaper than full re-review because the persona has already done the framing.
- **Cumulative trend over checkpoints.** Three or four checkpoints accumulate; the skill plots divergence-over-time. The first one is exploratory; the third one shows whether calibration is working.

**Recommended:** start with the focused-questions-on-divergence pattern (5–8 passages, yes/no/explain per passage), output a structured drift report, accumulate trend data across calls in a sidecar file. The 5–15 minute claim is *aspirational*; in practice the first checkpoint will probably take longer because the author needs to learn the format. Note this in the SKILL.md.

### 3. Cross-checks with voice-profile (#43) — what disagreement means

The composition: if `voice-profile` says the author hedges at a certain density and the persona is producing critiques that demand more aggressive claims, that's an independent drift signal — *without* the author having to weigh in. Three operational stances:

- **Voice-profile as silent prior.** The persona's outputs are scored against the profile in the background; large deviations get auto-flagged for the next calibration checkpoint.
- **Voice-profile as explicit input to the checkpoint.** The author is shown "the persona disagreed with you on §3, *and* its suggested rewrite drops hedges your voice profile says you typically use" — a triangulated signal.
- **Ignore voice-profile.** Treat the two skills as independent.

**Recommended:** explicit input. The whole point of the persona is to be a faithful surrogate; voice-profile is the most concrete operational definition of "faithful to this author" available. If persona feedback contradicts voice-profile, that *is* the drift signal; making it visible turns a checkpoint into a triangulation rather than a single-axis judgment.

### 4. Honest-about-disagreement framing — who's wrong?

The issue (#44) flags this: "Disagreement between persona and author is not necessarily a persona failure — sometimes the persona is right and the author was inconsistent." The drift report needs space for *both* directions of update: persona-spec edits AND author-declared-preference edits. Concretely, the report should let the author say "actually the persona is right; update my `known_weaknesses` / `core_claims` / `style.tone` to match." This is non-trivial because it touches the state file via a non-init skill; needs a careful, explicit author-consent step.

## Recommended scope cut for v0.3

Ship the **minimum-viable calibration loop** that:

1. Reads a persona spec, a manuscript artifact, and the persona's most-recent feedback on that artifact.
2. Reads the author's `voice-profile.extracted` if present (composition with #43).
3. Selects 5–8 passages where the persona's verdict diverges most from a baseline (voice-profile cross-check; rank-disagreement across persona instances; or simply highest persona-confidence critiques).
4. Walks the author through the selected passages with one focused question per passage.
5. Computes divergence metrics (set overlap, rank correlation; specific operationalization TBD per decision point 1).
6. Emits a markdown drift report **and** writes a structured `calibration-log.yaml` sidecar appending the new entry.
7. Produces a persona-spec diff for the author to accept or reject. Never auto-applies.
8. Produces a `MANUSCRIPT_STATE.yaml` diff for *author-declared-preference* edits, also for the author to accept or reject.

This deliberately scopes out fully-automated spec updates, cross-author calibration, and trend visualization beyond what the YAML record allows. v0.4 can extend.

## Acceptance criteria

- After three calibration cycles on a real test (the ten-simple-rules paper is the planned worked example per #44), the persona's feedback agreement with the author measurably improves on the chosen divergence metrics.
- The drift report fits on one screen (rule of thumb from #44).
- Every spec edit is a diff the author confirms; nothing is auto-applied.
- Trend data is structured (YAML) and can be plotted over time without re-parsing markdown.
- The 5–15 minute author-time target is met or honestly missed (the report's own header records elapsed time).
- The skill refuses to run without an existing persona spec and at least one round of persona feedback to calibrate against — no calibration-from-nothing.
- A platform-neutral `prompt.md` exists.
- Grounded explicitly in `author-role-evidence`, `ai-peer-review-research`, and (if #43 has shipped) `corpus-based-stylometry` via `voice-profile`.

## Open questions for the human

- Where does the persona spec live? In the repo (under version control, but with risk of leaking author-style detail)? In a sidecar gitignored file? In `MANUSCRIPT_STATE.yaml`?
- Is voice-profile (#43) a *required* dependency or a *strengthening* input? Required = persona-calibration can't ship until voice-profile does; strengthening = persona-calibration ships first with voice-profile as an optional cross-check.
- Should the drift report be calibration-checkpoint-numbered (calibration-001, 002, …) or by date? Numbering supports trend; date supports "when did this drift first appear."
- What's the right divergence metric? Jaccard-on-flagged-sections is cheap; rank correlation on critique priority is meaningful but assumes both sides rank; weighted disagreement (where the author marks critiques as load-bearing vs cosmetic) is most informative but most expensive.
- Does the calibration log get committed to the manuscript repo or held externally? Externally is safer (less leaked author signal); internally is more useful for the trend visualization story.
- If a third party (e.g. a co-author) runs the skill, does it refuse — same posture as reviewer-simulation's editorial-side refusal — or quietly produce a less-useful calibration? Calibration *for* an author *by* that author is an obvious author-side activity; calibration *of* another author's persona is murkier.
