# v0.3 design memo: `voice-profile`

*Status: design per issue [#43](https://github.com/seandavi/scriptorium/issues/43). Depends on [#42](https://github.com/seandavi/scriptorium/issues/42) (author-persona docs). This memo sharpens the design questions; it does not commit to a build.*

## What's ready

- **Author-roles grounding exists.** [`knowledge/author-roles/author-role-evidence.md`](../../knowledge/author-roles/author-role-evidence.md) is already load-bearing for the persona work — Sommers (1980), Geisler (1994), Swales' CARS, Hyland's stance/engagement model. The note explicitly anchors #42/#43/#44 and frames personas as differing on *attentional priority*, not only voice.
- **Voice-loss / AI-writing-failure-mode framing is solid.** [`knowledge/prior-art/ai-writing-failure-modes.md`](../../knowledge/prior-art/ai-writing-failure-modes.md) covers Kobak et al. 2024 (excess-vocabulary "ChatGPT smell") and the Agarwal et al. 2024 homogenization result. The note explicitly proposes (footnote: at the end of "Implementation priority") a `voice-fidelity-audit` skill as the condition that would flip voice work from documentation to skill.
- **Schema is permissive enough.** `MANUSCRIPT_STATE.yaml` already carries `style.tone`, `style.voice` (active/passive/mixed), `style.audience`, and `terminology.{preferred,forbidden,synonyms}`. These are declared inputs, not extracted ones, but they're the obvious composition target.
- **Hedging-preservation discipline is enforced in `argumentative-flow`.** Constraint 6 ("Hedging and stance markers are preserved") and the grounding in [`knowledge/scientific-writing/esl-writers-swales-hyland.md`](../../knowledge/scientific-writing/esl-writers-swales-hyland.md) mean the *consumer* side of a voice profile already knows what to do with stance-marker counts.

## What's missing

- **The corpus-based stylometry literature is not yet captured.** Hoover's vocabulary-richness and Delta-method work, Argamon's stylistic-feature taxonomy, and the Stamatatos 2009 survey on authorship attribution are the empirical bedrock for "can you extract a recognizable profile from a small corpus." Without a dedicated grounding note, the skill would be making claims it can't trace back. **A new knowledge note covering this is the recommended companion to this memo;** see [`knowledge/scientific-writing/corpus-based-stylometry.md`](../../knowledge/scientific-writing/corpus-based-stylometry.md) (drafted alongside this memo).
- **Schema slot `author_voice` does not exist.** Issue #43 proposes `MANUSCRIPT_STATE.yaml#author_voice`. This needs to be specified before any skill consumes the profile.
- **Boundary with the global `humanizer` skill is not yet documented.** The two skills sit on the same axis (stylistic features) in opposite directions; the conceptual relationship needs to be stated somewhere — likely in the SKILL.md grounding section, possibly in the new knowledge note.

## Architectural decision points

### 1. What's defensible from 3–5 single-author papers

This is the load-bearing empirical question. The stylometry literature is unambiguous that **authorship-attribution-from-corpus is a real, measurable signal at the population level** — Stamatatos (2009) reviews the field; Hoover's Delta and Burrows' Delta are the canonical methods; function-word frequencies, n-gram distributions, and sentence-length distributions discriminate authors well in adversarial conditions. **What's much less clear is whether a 3–5 paper single-author corpus produces a profile the *author themselves* recognizes as their voice.** Authorship attribution doesn't need that — it needs to discriminate one author from another. Voice-profile-for-self-recognition is a *harder* problem because the signal-to-noise on individual stylistic tics is lower and authors are notoriously poor judges of their own writing.

**Implication for design:** scope down to features that are (a) measurable from a small corpus, (b) defensible per the stylometry literature, and (c) directly consumable by an existing skill. Sentence-length distribution, hedge density, citation-placement patterns, paragraph-opening conventions — yes. "How the author handles counterarguments" — much harder; defer.

### 2. Declared vs extracted vs computed (the schema fork)

Three plausible shapes for the `author_voice` slot:

- **Declared only** (author writes their own profile by hand). Cheap; shallow self-report; loses the whole point of corpus extraction.
- **Extracted only** (skill writes the profile; author cannot override). Out of step with the declared-work-scope principle — the author owns their voice.
- **Three-layer** — `author_voice.declared` (author overrides), `author_voice.extracted` (skill's findings, timestamped), `author_voice.computed_at` (when the extraction ran). Skill reads declared first; extracted is reference; consumers (argumentative-flow) prefer declared over extracted on conflict.

**Recommended:** three-layer. Matches scriptorium's posture (the author always wins; the tool surfaces evidence). Adds two fields to the schema.

### 3. Boundary with the `humanizer` skill (same axis, opposite direction)

`humanizer` (a global, non-scriptorium skill) removes AI tells: em-dash overuse, "delve / underscore / intricate", rule-of-three, vague attributions. `voice-profile` extracts the author's signal: how *they* use em-dashes, what *their* vocabulary tics are, whether *they* use rule-of-three. The same stylistic axis; opposite information directions. Practical implications:

- A voice profile may legitimately contain features `humanizer` would flag as AI-isms (e.g. an author who genuinely writes with em-dashes). The profile's job is to record the signal, not to police it.
- An argumentative-flow pass that consumes a voice profile should *not* also run a humanizer pass on the output; that would mix two opposed objectives.
- The SKILL.md should declare this relationship explicitly so users don't confuse "your voice contains em-dashes" with "you write like AI."

### 4. Single-author corpus vs co-authored material

Co-authored papers introduce confounding voice signal. Three options:

- Refuse to ingest co-authored material. Simple; aligns with the issue's "weight single-author higher" instruction; loses signal from authors who rarely write single-author.
- Accept co-authored material with a confidence penalty. The profile reports per-feature confidence; co-authored evidence drags it down.
- Require the author to label corpus items as `single` / `co-authored-lead` / `co-authored-contributor` and weight accordingly.

**Recommended:** start with option 1 (single-author only) for v0.3; relax in v0.4 once the small-corpus calibration is solid.

## Recommended scope cut for v0.3

Ship a **read-only voice-profile extraction** that:

1. Takes a corpus pointer (paths or globs to single-authored prior writing).
2. Refuses to run on fewer than N=3 documents (or emits a sparse profile labeled `insufficient-corpus`).
3. Extracts the *defensible* features only: sentence-length distribution (mean, SD, quartiles); paragraph-length distribution; hedge density (epistemic markers per 1000 words; types of hedges per Hyland's stance taxonomy); citation-placement pattern (parenthetical vs narrative); section-opening pattern (claim-first vs context-first, where detectable); top-K author-distinctive vocabulary tokens via Burrows' Delta or a simpler relative-frequency comparison against a baseline corpus.
4. Emits a structured YAML profile suitable for `MANUSCRIPT_STATE.yaml#author_voice.extracted` plus a markdown report citing the specific corpus passages each finding hinges on.
5. Names what it did NOT extract: argumentative patterns it can't reliably measure, voice features below detection threshold for the corpus size, anything requiring more than 3–5 documents to anchor.

This deliberately avoids the harder argumentative-pattern claims and the cross-author baseline-construction problem. v0.4 can extend.

## Acceptance criteria

- Pointed at a 3–5 paper single-author corpus from the author themselves, the skill produces a profile the author recognizes as "yes, that's how I write" on the *defensible features* (sentence-length distribution, hedge density, etc.). Claims that go beyond defensible features are absent or labeled `low-confidence`.
- Pointed at fewer than 3 documents (or at material the skill cannot identify as single-author), the profile is sparse and labels itself `insufficient-corpus`; it does not confabulate. (Matches issue #43 acceptance.)
- Output is schema-valid against an `author_voice` schema extension and can be re-consumed by `argumentative-flow` (or other future consumers) without re-parsing markdown.
- Every profile finding cites which corpus passages support it.
- The profile is editable: every extracted pattern can be overridden by the author into the `author_voice.declared` block.
- A platform-neutral `prompt.md` exists.
- Grounded explicitly in `corpus-based-stylometry` (new note), `author-role-evidence`, `ai-writing-failure-modes`, `esl-writers-swales-hyland`.

## Open questions for the human

- Is the three-layer schema (`declared` / `extracted` / `computed_at`) the right shape, or is a sidecar file (`author_voice.yaml` alongside `MANUSCRIPT_STATE.yaml`) cleaner? Sidecar avoids bloating the state file but loses some declared-work-scope coherence.
- What's the right baseline corpus for Burrows' Delta / relative-frequency comparison? PubMed abstracts at large? A field-specific corpus? Refusing to do relative-frequency analysis at all?
- Should the skill detect non-author-owned tics — e.g. journal house-style imposed by copy-editing on prior published papers — and try to filter them? Or treat them as part of the signal?
- The "voice profile is consumed by argumentative-flow" composition has a real risk: an argumentative-flow pass biased toward an extracted profile could amplify quirks the author would have edited out on a fresh pass. Is there a mitigation other than persona-calibration (#44)?
- How does the skill behave on grant prose vs paper prose vs blog vs README — voices that may legitimately differ? Does the profile carry a `register` field, or do you run different profiles per register?
