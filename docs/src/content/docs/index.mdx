## Where scriptorium fits in the writing workflow

The seven shipped lifecycle stages in `MANUSCRIPT_STATE.yaml`
(`outline` → `draft` → `review` → `revision` → `submission` →
`post-submission` → `accepted`) map onto four roles in
[Hayes' 2012 cognitive-process model](https://github.com/seandavi/scriptorium/blob/main/knowledge/scientific-writing/hayes-flower-writing-model.md):
**proposer, translator, evaluator, transcriber**. Scriptorium occupies
the *translator and evaluator* roles when the author has already
proposed. It does not propose for the author.

<div class="workflow-stages">
  <div class="workflow-stages__card workflow-stages__card--out">
    <h3>Outline · drafting</h3>
    <p>The author owns hypothesis selection, framing, and the first prose pass.</p>
    <p class="workflow-stages__skills">Not what scriptorium is for. See <a href="./concepts/design/#workflow-scope--declared-work-not-blank-slate">scope</a>.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="workflow-stages__card">
    <h3>Revise · pressure-test</h3>
    <p>The author has draft prose and wants it tightened. Most scriptorium skills land here.</p>
    <p class="workflow-stages__skills">citation-audit, argumentative-flow, gap-finder, reviewer-simulation, terminology-normalization, figure-text-alignment</p>
  </div>
  <div class="workflow-stages__card">
    <h3>Pre-submission</h3>
    <p>Final compliance, venue fit, reviewer-anticipation checks.</p>
    <p class="workflow-stages__skills">desk-rejection-risk, venue-fit, reporting-guideline-fit, reporting-guideline-compliance, author-contribution-audit, compression</p>
  </div>
</div>

Skills do not auto-invoke. The author chooses which one to run and
when. The [skill reference](./reference/skills/) lists every shipped
skill with its category and the phases it expects.

## Four panels, one editorial layer

<div class="feature-band">
  <div class="feature-band__body">
    <p class="feature-band__layer">Panel 1 · what each skill does</p>
    <h2 class="feature-band__title">Structured, anchored findings</h2>
    <p class="feature-band__audience">If you want concrete output you can act on — not a quality score.</p>
    <p>
      Every critique skill emits a fixed-shape structured report.
      `reviewer-simulation` produces four lenses (methodological
      skeptic, domain expert, translational/clinical, statistical),
      each with Major Critiques / Minor Critiques / Fatal Concerns
      / Enthusiasm Drivers / Suggested Revisions / Acceptance Risk.
      Each finding is anchored to a specific sentence, paragraph, or
      section. No letter grade. No overall pass/fail.
    </p>
    <div class="feature-band__callout">
      <strong>If you've worried "AI critique" means crushing
      framing attacks:</strong> scriptorium's reviewer-simulation is
      grounded in <a href="https://github.com/seandavi/scriptorium/blob/main/knowledge/peer-review/common-critiques-taxonomy.md"><code>common-critiques-taxonomy</code></a>
      and produces fixable bench/stats tasks like "report the 95%
      CI for the AUC" or "Methods §2.3 omits the missing-data
      handling." You set <code>meta.guidance_level</code> to
      <code>terse</code>, <code>standard</code>, or <code>full</code>;
      the structure of the output does not change, but the framing
      around each elicited field does.
    </div>
    <p class="feature-band__cta">
      <a href="./concepts/case-study/">See a worked reviewer-simulation run →</a>
    </p>
  </div>
  <div class="feature-band__code">

```text
### Statistical lens

Major critiques.
- AUC=0.79 reported without
  95% CI. Add the CI.
- Internal validation missing
  (bootstrap optimism / k-fold).
  EPV is in range; the
  calibration step is not.

Minor critiques.
- Diabetes duration p<0.001
  reported without effect size.
  Add OR with 95% CI.

Fatal concerns. None.
Acceptance risk. Moderate.
```

  </div>
</div>

<div class="feature-band feature-band--reverse">
  <div class="feature-band__body">
    <p class="feature-band__layer">Panel 2 · what gets preserved</p>
    <h2 class="feature-band__title">A preservation contract, not a voice guarantee</h2>
    <p class="feature-band__audience">If "AI flattens my prose" is your worry — read this section closely.</p>
    <p>
      Critique skills (<code>citation-audit</code>,
      <code>reviewer-simulation</code>, <code>gap-finder</code>,
      <code>desk-rejection-risk</code>) do not rewrite prose at all.
      They emit structured reports. Your manuscript file is read,
      not modified.
    </p>
    <p>
      Transformation skills (<code>argumentative-flow</code>,
      <code>compression</code>) operate under an explicit preservation
      contract:
    </p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Citations.</strong> Every key in the source is preserved; none are added.</li>
      <li><strong>Statistics.</strong> Every numerical value, p-value, CI, and unit is preserved verbatim.</li>
      <li><strong>Declared terminology.</strong> Whatever you put in <code>terminology.preferred</code> / <code>forbidden</code> / <code>synonyms</code> is respected.</li>
      <li><strong>Hedging style and stance markers.</strong> The skill is required to report any shift in hedging strength as a per-edit note rather than absorbing it silently.</li>
      <li><strong>Declared core claims.</strong> No claim is added; no declared claim is dropped.</li>
    </ul>
    <div class="feature-band__callout">
      <strong>The honest caveat.</strong> Sentence-level "voice
      preservation" is not a guarantee scriptorium makes. The
      lexical-fingerprint evidence (Kobak et al. 2024) shows that
      LLM-edited prose is detectable at corpus scale; correcting
      it at sentence scale is not reliable. The preservation
      contract above is what scriptorium <em>does</em> guarantee.
      A general "this will sound like you" promise is what
      scriptorium <em>does not</em>. See
      <a href="https://github.com/seandavi/scriptorium/blob/main/knowledge/prior-art/ai-writing-failure-modes.md"><code>ai-writing-failure-modes</code></a>
      for the underlying evidence.
    </div>
    <p class="feature-band__cta">
      <a href="./concepts/design/#authorial-voice-loss--chatgpt-smell">Why scriptorium positions this way →</a>
    </p>
  </div>
  <div class="feature-band__code">

```yaml
# MANUSCRIPT_STATE.yaml
terminology:
  preferred:
    - "tumor-infiltrating
       lymphocytes (TILs)"
    - "anti-PD-1"
  forbidden:
    - "groundbreaking"
    - "novel"

constraints:
  preserve_citations: true
  preserve_statistics: true
  avoid_hype: true

style:
  voice: active
  tone:
    - quantitative
    - restrained
```

  </div>
</div>

<div class="feature-band">
  <div class="feature-band__body">
    <p class="feature-band__layer">Panel 3 · what the state file does</p>
    <h2 class="feature-band__title">One file, every skill reads it</h2>
    <p class="feature-band__audience">If you want to control what skills know about your work.</p>
    <p>
      <code>MANUSCRIPT_STATE.yaml</code> lives at the root of your
      manuscript repository. Every scriptorium skill reads it. It
      records what the manuscript is arguing
      (<code>core_claims</code>), the limitations you have already
      chosen to acknowledge (<code>known_weaknesses</code>; see
      <a href="./reference/manuscript-state-schema/#a-note-on-known_weaknesses">naming note</a>),
      preferred / forbidden terminology, declared style and audience,
      and constraint flags.
    </p>
    <div class="feature-band__callout">
      <strong>File scope.</strong> <code>MANUSCRIPT_STATE.yaml</code>
      lives in your repository. It is not uploaded to scriptorium
      and is not shared with anyone unless you commit it to a
      shared branch. Whether to commit it (private fork; private
      git remote; <code>.gitignore</code>) is your call. The
      reviewer-simulation skill reads
      <code>known_weaknesses</code> so it can <em>refrain</em> from
      flagging items you have already acknowledged — the field is a
      calibration input, not a disclosure target.
    </div>
    <p class="feature-band__cta">
      <a href="./reference/manuscript-state-schema/">Schema reference →</a>
      &nbsp;·&nbsp;
      <a href="https://github.com/seandavi/scriptorium/blob/main/templates/MANUSCRIPT_STATE.example.yaml">Worked example →</a>
    </p>
  </div>
  <div class="feature-band__code">

```yaml
project:
  title: "..."
  target_venue: "Nature Cancer"

document_phase:
  current: revision

core_claims:
  - "Shorter diabetes duration
     and lower pre-op HbA1c
     predict remission..."

known_weaknesses:
  - "Single-center retrospective
     design."
  - "No external validation
     cohort."

meta:
  guidance_level: standard
```

  </div>
</div>

    <p class="feature-band__layer">Panel 4 · where it grounds</p>
    <h2 class="feature-band__title">Curated, peer-reviewed evidence</h2>
    <p class="feature-band__audience">If you want to know what every design choice is based on.</p>
    <p>
      Every skill cites the knowledge notes its design decisions
      come from. Fifty-plus notes cover citation accuracy,
      hallucination evidence, reporting-guideline density (CONSORT,
      STROBE, PRISMA, ARRIVE, TRIPOD+AI, CONSORT-AI / SPIRIT-AI),

## What scriptorium will not do

A pre-submission tool earns trust by being explicit about its
limits. These are not aspirations — they are the
[explicit non-goals](https://github.com/seandavi/scriptorium/blob/main/docs/roadmap.md#explicit-non-goals)
in the roadmap, each pointing at the evidence note that justifies it.

<div class="limits-grid">
  <div class="limits-grid__column">
    <h3>Out of scope by design</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>No blank-slate prose generation.</strong> Scriptorium does not draft sections from nothing. The proposer role (Hayes 2012) is yours. <a href="https://github.com/seandavi/scriptorium/blob/main/knowledge/conventions/declared-work-scope.md">declared-work-scope</a>.</li>
      <li><strong>No autonomous reviewing.</strong> <code>reviewer-simulation</code> is author-side only. Editorial-side use violates ICMJE, NIH, and major-publisher policy.</li>
      <li><strong>No overall quality score.</strong> No letter grade, no Flesch-Kincaid, no "writing quality" number. Those systematically misrate scientific prose.</li>
      <li><strong>No reference-manager replacement.</strong> Citation audit works with the bibliography Zotero / Mendeley / BibTeX already produced. <a href="https://github.com/seandavi/scriptorium/blob/main/knowledge/prior-art/reference-managers.md">reference-managers</a>.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="limits-grid__column">
    <h3>Out of scope at this stage</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>No image forensics.</strong> Bik-style figure-integrity review and tortured-phrase detection require domain experts. Scriptorium is a pre-submission first pass, not a sleuth.</li>
      <li><strong>No discipline defaults outside biomedical / clinical (v0.1–v0.3).</strong> The evidence base is biomedical-coded. Physics, CS/ML, mathematics, humanities defaults need per-discipline knowledge layers that don't exist yet.</li>
      <li><strong>No native .docx round-trip.</strong> Scriptorium operates on markdown-converted prose. Tracked changes and field codes are not preserved through the conversion.</li>
      <li><strong>No skill-degradation defence.</strong> Whether extended use atrophies the author's underlying skill is plausible but under-evidenced. Author retains responsibility for their writing.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

Skills also surface their own limits in the report itself —
each output includes a "What this skill did NOT check" section so
the limit is in front of the author at decision time, not buried
in documentation.
